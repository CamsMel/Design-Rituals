# Ask Me Anything 🎤 — external guest

**The promise.** Someone from outside Thiga comes to talk: a sector we never
see (games, furniture, fashion, a startup) or a craft we don't have in house.

**Format.** In person, 60 min. 20 min of guest, 30 min of questions, then a
lightning round and the close. Title + 4 slides — that is the whole deck.
Template: `assets/templates/ask-me-anything.pptx` · `--template ask-me-anything`

**The trap.** Letting it turn into a fifty-minute talk with five rushed minutes
at the end. The name of the ritual is a clue.

**The guest builds nothing unless they feel like it.** The host's job is four
slides, twelve questions, and a room that never goes quiet.

## Two things this skill produces beyond the deck

1. **The bank of 12 questions**, split across the four buckets on slide 4:
   Context (their world, day to day) · Craft (process, artefacts, what they
   dropped) · Friction (what's hard, what failed) · Transfer (what we'd need to
   know walking into their sector). Build twelve, expect to use six. Deliver
   them as a list alongside the deck, not crammed onto the slide.
2. **The question-collection message** for the tribe's Slack, to post a week
   ahead. Short, names the guest, says what they've built, asks for questions
   with a deadline. Run it through `tone-of-voice` then `anti-ai-writing`, and
   write it in the language the tribe's channel actually uses.

## Slide plan

**1. Cover — `ASK ME ANYTHING 🎤`** · 3 min
`[DATE]` · `[Guest name]` · `[role]` · `[company]` · `[Your name]`
You're hosting, not presenting. Say the split out loud at minute three: twenty
minutes for the guest, the rest for the room. It tells the guest when to stop
and gives everyone permission to jump in.

**2. `WHY WE INVITED THEM`** · 2 min
`[What they've built]` · `[Why it matters to us right now]` ·
`[One surprising thing, from your prep call rather than their LinkedIn]` ·
`[guest]` in the "Today: 20 minutes from [guest], then 30 minutes of your
questions" line. Guest photo zone on the right.
Ninety seconds. Don't read their CV, say why they're here today. The surprising
detail is what warms the room up.

**3. `OUR ANCHOR QUESTIONS`** · 30 min
`[Something only this guest can answer]` ×3 — pass a 3-item list.
Leave this slide up through the Q&A: it gives the room something to build on and
kills the awkward first silence. Read out the name of whoever sent a question;
people write better questions when they get the credit.

**4. `LIGHTNING ROUND`** · 8 min
FOUR QUICK ONES: `[…]` ×4 — pass a 4-item list.
The right-hand column already names the four buckets; the bank of 12 goes in the
handover message, not on the slide.

**5. `STAY IN TOUCH`** · 4 min
`[Guest]` · `[newsletter, portfolio, LinkedIn]` · `[link]` (Resources) ·
`[guest name]` in the thank-you line.
Send the thank-you the same day, with the recording and five bullets in the
tribe space.

## Interview

1. Who's the guest, what have they built, and why them now?
2. What came out of your 20-minute prep call — including what's off-limits and
   whether we can record. If you haven't had the call yet, that's the one thing
   to do before anything else.
3. The one surprising thing you learned about them that isn't on their LinkedIn.
4. Which themes does the tribe want to hear them on?
5. Who's keeping time? It shouldn't be you.

## Quality gates

- **Zero closed questions in the whole hour.** Every question starts with how,
  what, or "tell us about the time…". A yes/no question buys ten seconds of
  answer and kills the rhythm. Check all sixteen (3 anchor + 4 lightning + the
  bank of 12 minus overlaps).
- Are the anchor questions unanswerable by anybody else? "What does design
  maturity look like in a fifteen-person games studio?" passes. "What advice
  would you give juniors?" doesn't.
- Is a timekeeper named, and is it not the host?
- Did the prep call happen, and is the surprising detail from it rather than
  from LinkedIn?
- Is the deck still 5 slides? This is the format where the temptation to add
  guest slides is strongest. The guest brings their own or nothing.
- Is the Slack collection message written and dated a week ahead?
