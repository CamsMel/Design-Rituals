# Let's Work(shop) 🧰 — co-animated workshop

**The promise.** A workshop run with one of the thematic Tribes (AI, BtoE,
Responsible Product) on something they, or Thiga, actually need moved forward.

**Format.** In person, 60 min, co-animated. 8 slides.
Template: `assets/templates/lets-workshop.pptx` · `--template lets-workshop`

**The trap.** Cramming in three exercises. And the quieter trap: an output with
no owner. An output nobody carries is a pleasant hour that evaporates, and that
is exactly what makes people stop signing up.

## Design it backwards

Not a suggestion — the order the interview follows:

**the output → who owns it → the exercise → the framing → the slides.**

Slide 2 ("AT 14:00 WE WALK OUT WITH…") gets written before anything else. If it
takes more than one sentence, the workshop isn't designed yet, and no amount of
slide work will fix that. Say this to the consultant plainly rather than
producing a deck around a vague output.

The slides here are closer to instructions posted on a wall than to a talk
support. Write them as things the room reads while doing something, not as
things a speaker narrates.

## Slide plan

**1. Cover — `LET'S WORK(SHOP) 🧰`** · 1 min
`[DATE]` · `[Workshop title]` · `[thematic Tribe]` · `[Your name]` ·
`[Client or anonymised descriptor]`
Co-animated, so agree in writing beforehand: who frames, who runs the exercise,
who watches the clock, who owns the output.

**2. `AT 14:00 WE WALK OUT WITH…`** · written first
`[The output, in one sentence]`
A ranked list, a filled canvas, five tested prompts, a decision. Nothing else
goes on this slide. Said out loud twice: now, and again before the share-back.

**3. `WHY THIS, WHY NOW`** · 3 min
`[The thematic Tribe objective this serves]` ·
`[Or the Thiga objective this serves]` ·
`[What happens to the output afterwards]` ·
`[co-animator, thematic Tribe]` (Delivered by)
The co-animator delivers this one. It's their legitimacy, and it's the
visibility on cross-Thiga work the tribe asked for.

**4. `THE QUESTION WE'RE WORKING ON`** · 2 min
`[One question. One slide.]`
Big type, no sub-questions. Sub-questions belong in the exercise instructions.

**5. `HOW IT WORKS`** · 2 min — a 5×3 table (TIME / WHAT / HOW)
Ships with a worked schedule: solo silent → table work → 90-second share-back →
decisions and owners. Rewrite the times to the real slot via the `tables` fill.
Leave it up during the solo round; it answers most of "wait, what are we doing?"
without anyone interrupting. The silence in the solo round is a facilitation
choice — hold it, kindly.

**6. `THE CANVAS`** · 20 min — a 4×4 table
`[COLUMN 1..4]` heads, then `[your example]` ×4 for the example row, then two
empty rows for the tables to fill.
The highest-leverage slide of the kit: one row filled in by the facilitator. It
cuts the "what do you mean exactly?" phase from six minutes to nothing. Make the
example deliberately average so nobody feels they have to match it.
One facilitator circulates during table work, the other stays at the front.
Both circulating means nobody is watching the clock.

**7. `SHARE-BACK RULES`** · 12 min
Ships with the rules — 90 seconds per table, timer visible, three outputs, no
re-contextualising. Fill `[name]` for the scribe.
Eight tables at ninety seconds is twelve minutes with zero slack. Warn them
you'll cut them off, then do it on the first table.

**8. `SO WHAT HAPPENS NEXT`** · 8 min — a 3×4 table
DECISION / OWNER / BY WHEN / WHERE IT LANDS. Filled in live, in front of the
room. Leave `[…]`, `[name]`, `[date]`, `[link]` standing on purpose — this table
is written during the session, not before it.
An empty owner column is how a good workshop quietly turns into nothing.

## Interview

1. What precise output do people walk out with? One sentence. If you can't say
   it in one, we design that first and the slides come after.
2. Which thematic Tribe are you co-animating with, and what objective of theirs
   or of Thiga's does this serve?
3. The question the room works on.
4. Who owns the output afterwards, and where does it land?
5. How many tables do you expect, and what's the real time slot?

## Quality gates

- **One exercise.** If a second one appeared, cut it. With forty people, one
  exercise run properly beats three run at a sprint.
- Was slide 2 written before everything else, and does it fit in one sentence?
- Is the canvas example row deliberately average? A brilliant example
  intimidates the room into copying it.
- Does the canvas exist printed *and* digital, for the remote joiners?
- Is the owner column ready to be filled in front of the room, with real names
  available?
- Does the schedule fit in 60 minutes with the share-back included? 8 tables ×
  90 sec = 12 minutes with no margin. Do the arithmetic on the real table count
  and say the number out loud.
- Do the slides read like instructions on a wall, or like a talk?
