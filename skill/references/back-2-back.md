# Back 2 Back 🕺💃 — two speakers, one question

**The promise.** Two consultants answer the same question about their missions,
side by side, so the room can see how much the context changes the answer.

**Format.** Remote, 30 min, two speakers. 7 slides.
Template: `assets/templates/back-2-back.pptx` · `--template back-2-back`

**The trap.** Smoothing both answers into a single tidy narrative. The friction
is the subject. Where they disagree, put it on the slide.

## How to run the interview differently here

This ritual has a facilitation rule that changes how you work: **get each grid
filled separately, and only compare afterwards.** Comparing too early flattens
exactly the differences that make the session worth attending. If you're talking
to both consultants at once, ask them to answer in separate messages without
reading each other's first. If you're talking to one, fill their side and leave
the other side bracketed.

Offer the context table as a 15-minute job to do together before the session. It
replaces two context slides, and it's the one the room screenshots.

If the two answers come back looking identical, don't paper over it — say so and
push: same job, different context, opposite strategy. There is almost always a
real difference hiding in tooling, seniority or team composition.

## Slide plan

**1. Cover — `BACK 2 BACK 🕺💃`** · 1 min
`[DATE]` · `[The one question you both answer]` ·
`[Your name · Your client]` — one token, printed twice ("A x B"), so pass a
2-item list and the two speakers land in the right order.
Whoever opens reads the question out loud, then stops talking. Without a written
question both default to "here's my mission and my pain points", which is a
T'REX with two speakers.

**2. `TWO CONTEXTS, SIDE BY SIDE`** · 3 min — an 8×3 table, `[SPEAKER A]` /
`[SPEAKER B]` as column heads. Rows: Client · Sector / Activity Type · Scope ·
Design maturity · Team composition · On this mission since… · Tooling (AI,
Research, Craft…). All cells are `[…]` — use the `tables` fill with full rows.
Read across the rows rather than down the columns; the contrast does the framing.

**3. `[SPEAKER A]'S ANSWER`** · 4 min
`[Your answer, one headline sentence]`, then WHERE IT'S STUCK `[…]` `[…]` and
WHAT'S WORKING `[…]` `[…]` — pass a 4-item list to `[…]` (stuck column first).

**4. `[SPEAKER B]'S ANSWER`** · 4 min
Identical layout, deliberately. Identical structure is what makes the comparison
readable — never redesign this one to differentiate it.

**5. `SAME`** · 2 min
`[What you both do, despite everything above]` plus two `[…]`
Usually the shortest slide, and the most reassuring one for whoever in the room
is two months into their first mission. Keep it short on purpose.

**6. `DIFFERENT`** · 3 min — a 4×3 table
`[The difference]` rows against `[SPEAKER A]` / `[SPEAKER B]` columns, then a
final row "Why the context explains it". The `[…]` cells run 6 deep across the
table — easier to fill this one with `tables` rows than with a long list.
The payoff slide. Leave the disagreement standing: "I run a weekly design
review, she thinks it would be pure theatre at her client" is a better slide
than a compromise nobody believes.

**7. `WHAT WE'D PINCH FROM EACH OTHER`** · 2 min, then questions
`A → B: [one thing]` · `B → A: [one thing]` · `[the question]` — the same
question thrown to the tribe, answers in the chat.

## Interview

1. The one question you're both answering. Write it as a question, not a theme.
2. Your context lines — client, sector, scope, design maturity, team, how long
   you've been there, tooling. (Ask each speaker separately.)
3. Your answer to the question, in one headline sentence.
4. Where it's stuck for you, and what's working. Two of each.
5. Reading the other's answer now: one thing you'd pinch from them.

## Quality gates

- Do slides 3 and 4 keep the same layout? If one got redesigned, put it back.
- Was each grid filled separately before comparing? If both were written
  together, the differences are probably already flattened.
- Does a real disagreement survive on slide 6, without a face-saving
  compromise?
- Is `SAME` short? A long SAME slide usually means the contexts weren't
  different enough to be worth the format.
- Do the answers stay in two voices? Never merge them into one narrator.
- Is the question on the cover a question the tribe can also answer in the chat?
