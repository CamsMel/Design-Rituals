# T'REX 🦖 — mission feedback

**The promise.** A slice of a mission, or the whole mission: the context, the
problem you hit, the approach you took, and what it changed for the client and
for you.

**Format.** Remote, 30 min. 18 min talking, 10 min questions. 7 slides.
Template: `assets/templates/trex.pptx` · `--template trex`

**The trap.** Telling the mission in chronological order. Six months never fit
into 18 minutes, and the good part ends up squeezed onto the last slide while
everyone's stomach rumbles. Pick one problem, one turning point, one outcome.
The rest is what the questions are for.

## Slide plan

Titles are fixed by the template. Tokens are exact — copy them into the JSON.

**1. Cover — `T'REX 🦖`** · 30 sec
`[DATE]` · `[The story you want to tell, in one line]` · `[Your name]` ·
`[Client or anonymised descriptor]`
The line is a hook, not a mission title. "Getting a fifteen-year-old back-office
team to talk to users again" makes people stay; "REX mission X" makes them open
Slack.

**2. `WHERE I LANDED`** · 2 min — a 5×2 table, four facts
`[e.g. online bank, 15 designers, part of a big group]` (Sector & size) ·
`[what it does, who uses it]` (The product) ·
`[a squad, a central team, on my own]` (Where I sat) ·
`[first designer ever? one of thirty? nobody had met a user?]` (Design maturity)
Design maturity is the line everyone actually wants. First designer in the
building or the thirtieth changes how the whole story reads.

**3. `THE BRIEF VS. THE ACTUAL PROBLEM`** · 3 min — two columns
Left, WHAT I WAS ASKED FOR: `[what the mission order said]` ·
`[what they thought they needed]`
Right, WHAT WAS REALLY GOING ON: `[what you found in week two]` ·
`[how you found out]`
Where a T'REX earns its slot. The moment of discovery is the story: the
interview nobody had run, the dashboard nobody had opened.

**4. `WHAT I DID ABOUT IT`** · 5 min — three moves, each with its artefact
`[Move 1, and the artefact that carried it]` ·
`[Move 2, and the artefact that carried it]` ·
`[Move 3, only if it deserves the airtime]` — pass `null` to drop it
Leaves a dashed zone for the artefacts. Show the artefact rather than naming the
method: everyone knows what a blueprint is, they want to see theirs, at that
client, under those constraints.

**5. `WHAT CHANGED`** · 3 min
`[The outcome, in the client's own words, one sentence]`
HARD EVIDENCE: `[a decision that got taken]` ·
`[a metric that moved, relative not absolute]`
SOFT EVIDENCE: `[an artefact the team kept using]` ·
`[a habit that survived after you left]`
"The team runs its own interviews now" lands better than a satisfaction score.
No number → say so. Never manufacture one.

**6. `WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY`** · 2 min
`[Thing 1, specific enough that someone could act on it]` · `[Thing 2, same]`
The slide colleagues reuse. "I'd have refused to start the workshop until the PO
was in the room", not "I'd manage my time better."

**7. `STEAL THIS`** · 2 min, then questions
`[Takeaway 1]` · `[Takeaway 2]` · `[Takeaway 3]` · `[your question to the tribe]`
A good closing question: "Who else has landed somewhere with no analytics at
all, and how did you build a baseline?" A bad one: "Any questions?"

## Interview

Five questions, one block. Skip any the consultant already answered in a brief
or notes, and say which ones you took from there.

1. The client in one line, plus design maturity — were you the first designer
   they'd ever worked with, or one of thirty?
2. What were you asked for, and what turned out to actually be going on? How did
   you find out?
3. Your 2 or 3 moves, and for each one the artefact you could show — a
   screenshot, a workshop photo, a map, a prototype.
4. What changed. A decision, a habit, a metric if you have one. If you don't
   have a number, say so — I'll leave it blank rather than invent it.
5. What you'd do differently, concrete enough that a colleague could act on it.

## Quality gates

- Do the two columns on slide 3 say genuinely different things? If both say the
  same, the story is somewhere else in the mission — go and find it.
- Does every move on slide 4 have an artefact to show, or is it a method name?
- Is the evidence relative and anonymised? No absolute client metric, no name.
- Is "what I'd do differently" concrete enough that a colleague could apply it
  next Monday?
- Does the closing question invite an answer rather than politeness?
