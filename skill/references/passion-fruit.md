# Passion Fruit 🍍 — a passion, and what it teaches

**The promise.** The most relaxed slot of the month. You talk about something
you love, what it quietly taught you as a designer, and everyone gets to know
each other a bit better. No need to be an expert.

**Format.** Remote, 30 min. 6 slides.
Template: `assets/templates/passion-fruit.pptx` · `--template passion-fruit`

**The trap.** Forcing the parallel. If the passion is bread and the claim is
that it taught you agile, everybody can tell. Two honest bridges beat three
invented ones — and "it taught me nothing about work, I just love it" is an
allowed answer that makes a better session than a manufactured one.

## Slide plan

**1. Cover — `PASSION FRUIT 🍍`** · 30 sec
`[DATE]` · `[Your passion, in three words]` · `[Your name]` ·
`[Client or anonymised descriptor]`
A photo of them practising it. Even a bad one.

**2. `HOW I GOT INTO IT`** · 2 min
`[When and how it started]` · `[Who or what dragged you in]` ·
`[Where you're at now: hours a week, level, gear]`
Photo zone on the right. Keep it short — the origin story is the warm-up, not
the session.

**3. `HOW IT ACTUALLY WORKS`** · 4 min
`[The one rule or mechanism outsiders never see]` ·
`[How it works, step one]` · `[How it works, step two]` ·
`[The vocabulary, defined the first time you use it]`
Plus a dashed zone for a diagram or photo of the thing being described.
Assume the room knows nothing, and explain one mechanism properly rather than
five in passing. Watch the jargon: they know far more about this than they
think.

**4. `SHOW, DON'T TELL`** · 4 min
A full dashed zone: best photos, a 60-second video, or an object held up to the
camera. Holding something up to a webcam is unreasonably good television, so
push for the object. Mute the video unless the sound is the point.
No tokens on this slide — it's a visual to drop in. Say so in the handover.

**5. `WHAT IT TAUGHT ME THAT I USE AT WORK`** · 4 min — a 4×2 table
IN MY PASSION `[the habit]` → IN MY DESIGN PRACTICE
`[something you did last month because of it]`, three rows. Pass 3-item lists,
`null` for a row that isn't real.
The slide to get right. Anchor each bridge in a real recent behaviour:
"climbing taught me to read a route before touching it, so I sketch three
approaches before opening Figma." Not "it taught me patience."

**6. `TRY IT YOURSELF`** · 2 min, then questions
`[first step]` (How to start this weekend) · `[gear, subscription, club]`
(What it costs) · `[book, channel, place, person]` (One resource) ·
`[your question to the tribe]`
A good closing question: "Who else has a hobby with a strict rulebook, and does
it make you more or less patient with design systems?"

## Interview

1. The passion in three words, and how you got into it in two lines.
2. The one mechanism outsiders never see — the thing you'd have to explain
   before anyone could follow you. Plus any vocabulary I should define.
3. Two or three things it taught you that you actually use at work. For each, a
   specific thing you did in the last month or so because of it. If a bridge
   feels forced while you're saying it, drop it.
4. What you can show: photos, a short video, an object you'd hold up to camera.
5. How someone would start this weekend — first step, what it costs, one
   resource.

## Quality gates

- The bridges slide is the one to get right: does each bridge cite a dated,
  verifiable behaviour rather than a virtue? "Patience", "rigour" and
  "creativity" are not bridges.
- Is jargon defined the first time it appears?
- Is the video under 60 seconds? On a remote call a minute of footage feels like
  four.
- Is there something to actually show, or is slide 4 still empty? Flag it in the
  handover — it's the slide that carries the session.
- Does the closing question open a conversation rather than close the deck?
