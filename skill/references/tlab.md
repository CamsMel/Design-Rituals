# T'LAB 🔬 — tool demo

**The promise.** AI tooling, craft, a tool found recently. Hands-on enough that
the room can go and redo it themselves.

**Format.** Remote, 30 min. 6 slides.
Template: `assets/templates/tlab.pptx` · `--template tlab`

**The trap.** Replaying the vendor demo. Everyone can watch the marketing video
on their own time. The value sits in running the tool on something the
consultant actually had to deliver: a discovery synthesis, a component audit, an
anonymised client deck.

## Slide plan

**1. Cover — `T'LAB 🔬`** · 30 sec
`[DATE]` · `[Tool name]` · `[the job it does, in one sentence]` ·
`[Your name]` · `[Client or anonymised descriptor]`
Describe the job, not the category. "Turns eight interview recordings into a
tagged verbatim bank" beats "an AI research tool".

**2. `THE PAIN IT TAKES AWAY`** · 2 min — a 4×2 table
`[the thing you used to do by hand]` (The task) ·
`[how long it took, how much it hurt]` (Before) ·
`[how long it takes now, honestly]` (After)
then `[be specific]` — Who in this room has this problem
If the honest "after" is "about the same, but less boring", that goes on the
slide. Still a useful finding.

**3. `GETTING SET UP`** · 2 min — a 5×2 table
`[free tier? per seat? per month?]` (Price) ·
`[account needed? SSO? already paid for at Thiga?]` (Access) ·
`[what it does with what you feed it, and what you don't know]` (Client data) ·
`[the two others you looked at]` (Alternatives)
Never skip the data line. Half the room is quietly wondering whether they can
point this at a client's files. "I have no idea what they do with the data" is a
fine answer and a useful warning. Guessing is not.

**4. `LIVE DEMO`** · 12 min
`[Step 1, what you'll show]` · `[Step 2, where it gets interesting]` ·
`[Step 3, the output]`
Plus a dashed zone for a screenshot of the end result — the parachute for the
day the demo dies live, and one day it will.

**5. `VERDICT`** · 3 min — two columns
WHERE IT SHINES and WHERE IT FALLS APART both hold two `[…]` bullets each; pass
a 4-item list to the `[…]` token so the occurrences fill in document order (left
column first).
Then `[be blunt]` — What it does not replace.
The right-hand column is what earns trust. A tool with no listed limits reads
like an ad, and the room quietly discounts everything else.

**6. `STEAL THIS`** · 2 min, then questions
`[paste the real one, or the link]` (the prompt or config) ·
`[link]` (the template) · `[one step]` (where I'd start if I were you) ·
`[your question to the tribe]`
The slide people come back to weeks later. The actual prompt goes on it, not a
polite summary.

## Interview

1. Which real task did you point it at — something you had to deliver, not a toy
   example? How long did it take before, how long now, honestly?
2. Price, access, and what the tool does with what you feed it. Say what you
   don't know, that's an answer too.
3. The three steps of your demo, in order.
4. Two places it falls apart. Blunt ones.
5. The prompt or config you actually used — paste it as-is, I'll put the real
   text on the slide.

## Quality gates

- Is the task on slide 2 a task the tribe genuinely does? If not, the tool may
  be interesting but it isn't a T'LAB.
- Is the client-data line filled, including the part the consultant doesn't
  know?
- Is the limits column as full as the strengths column? A thin right-hand column
  is the tell of a demo that became an advert.
- Is the prompt pasted verbatim rather than summarised politely?
- Is there a screenshot of the end result as a fallback for the live demo?
