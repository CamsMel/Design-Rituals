# That's a Banger 🥇 — design outside digital

**The promise.** A short exposé on design that has nothing to do with our
screens, so the tribe can pinch ideas from other domains and from people working
under very different constraints.

**Format.** Remote, 30 min. 7 slides.
Template: `assets/templates/thats-a-banger.pptx` · `--template thats-a-banger`

**The trap.** The Wikipedia summary. Dates and a timeline are not a banger. What
works: two or three deliberate decisions, the option that got rejected, and the
constraint that forced the call.

## Slide plan

**1. Cover — `THAT'S A BANGER 🥇`** · 30 sec
`[DATE]` · `[The object, typeface, trend or piece of industrial design]` ·
`[Your name]` · `[Client or anonymised descriptor]`
One full-bleed image behind the text. The shot that makes someone say "oh, that
thing". Technical photos come later.

**2. `WHY THIS ONE`** · 1 min
`[Your hook, one sentence, in your own voice]`
Personal works best: "I've walked past this every day for four years and never
noticed the handrail was doing three jobs at once."

**3. `THE BRIEF BEHIND IT`** · 3 min — a 5×2 table
`[designer, studio, agency, nobody knows]` (Who made it) ·
`[year, country, context]` (When & where) ·
`[budget · material · regulation · era · audience]` (The constraint) ·
`[why it had to work]` (What was at stake)
The constraint carries the story. Design with no constraint is decoration; what
we want is how somebody worked inside a limit. If a fact is shaky, keep the
hedge — "apparently" belongs on the slide as much as in the room.

**4. `DECISION ONE, DECODED`** · 3 min
`[what they did]` · `[what they didn't do]` (REJECTED) ·
`[the constraint that decided it]` (WHY)
Image zone on the right, close enough to see. One image, three boxes, one line
each. Needing a paragraph means drifting into explaining the object instead of
the decision.

**5. `DECISION TWO, DECODED`** · 3 min
Same three tokens, same layout, deliberately. Pass the values as a 2-item list
per token across slides 4 and 5, or fill them slide by slide.

**6. `THE DETAIL NOBODY NOTICES`** · 2 min
`[What it's for, in one line]` plus a dashed zone for one tight crop — kerning,
a weld, a colour that only exists at night, a curve that stops water pooling.
The crowd-pleaser: the slide people screenshot and send to a friend who doesn't
work in design.

**7. `WHAT WE CAN PINCH`** · 3 min, then questions — a 3×2 table
IN THEIR WORLD `[the principle]` → IN OURS
`[the product design equivalent, concrete]`, two rows. Pass 2-item lists.
Then `[your question]` and `[book, doc, archive]` (Sources).

## Interview

1. What's the object, and what's your personal hook — why this one, in your own
   words?
2. Who made it, when, and under what constraint? Budget, material, regulation,
   era, audience.
3. Two deliberate decisions. For each: what they did, what they rejected, and
   the constraint that decided it.
4. The detail nobody notices — the thing you'd zoom into.
5. What we can pinch, translated into something concrete on a product. And your
   sources.

## Quality gates

- Two decisions decoded properly, not four name-dropped. Cut before you add.
- Does each decision name the option that got rejected? Without the rejected
  option it's a description, not a decision.
- Is the transfer concrete — "design the empty state for someone who is already
  late" rather than "think about context"?
- Are the sources credited on the last slide?
- Is the last slide a real question to the tribe, not a thank-you?
